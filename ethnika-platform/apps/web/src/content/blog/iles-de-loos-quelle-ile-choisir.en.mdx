Half an hour by canoe from Conakry, the Loos archipelago shows three very different faces. Portuguese navigators called it *Ilhas dos Ídolos*, the "islands of idols". British in the 19th century, the islands were ceded to France in 1904. Today they are the favourite weekend getaway of Conakry's residents.

## Getting there

Motorised canoes leave from Boulbinet harbour, in Kaloum. Allow about 30 minutes for the crossing, depending on the sea. A life jacket is essential: our crossings always include one.

## Kassa, the lively one

The busiest island: beach restaurants, music and a Saturday-night atmosphere. It is also home to the remains of the Fotoba penitentiary, best visited with a guide to understand the archipelago's colonial history.

## Tamara, the wild one

The largest of the islands wins visitors over with its red-sand beaches and Alcatraz rock, a refuge for seabirds. Ideal for walking, photography and watching wildlife.

> "Tamara's red sand at sunset is an image you never forget."

## Roume, the peaceful one

Small and quiet, Roume is perfect for resting, reading and swimming with the family, far from the noise of the capital.
